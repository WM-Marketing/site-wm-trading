Import by account and order and custom import are both forms of indirect import into Brazil, but they are not interchangeable. The central difference is who makes the international purchase, where the financial resources originate, and the commercial relationship between the trading company, the foreign supplier, and the Brazilian company.

In an import by account and order operation, the acquiring company is the effective importer and hires a trading company to perform the operation on its behalf. In a custom import operation, the trading company purchases the goods with its own resources and subsequently resells them to a predetermined ordering company. The chosen model must reflect the commercial, financial, and documentary reality of the operation.

## What is import by account and order?

In an import by account and order operation, the acquiring company purchases the goods abroad and hires a trading company to carry out customs clearance and related services in its own name. The financial resources originate from the acquiring company, even when the trading company makes payments to the foreign supplier as part of the contracted service.

According to Brazil's <a href="https://www.gov.br/receitafederal/pt-br/assuntos/aduana-e-comercio-exterior/manuais/despacho-de-importacao/topicos-1/importacao-por-conta-e-ordem-e-importacao-por-encomenda-1/importacao-por-conta-e-ordem/o-que-e-a-importacao-por-conta-e-ordem" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Federal Revenue Service</a>, the acquiring company is the principal in the transaction and the trading company acts as its agent. ## What is custom import?

In a custom import operation, the trading company is hired to import, in its own name and with its own resources, goods acquired abroad for resale to a predetermined ordering company. In this model, the international purchase and sale relationship occurs between the trading company and the foreign supplier; after customs clearance, the trading company sells the goods in Brazil to the ordering company.

## How do you choose the import model?

The choice should not be based solely on tax cost or operational convenience. The model needs to match the actual flow of funds, the commercial relationship between the parties, and the responsibilities each company assumes.

In general, import by account and order may be appropriate when the acquiring company negotiates the international purchase and provides the resources for the operation, but hires a trading company to handle customs clearance, logistics, and nationalization. Custom import may be appropriate when the trading company purchases with its own resources in order to resell the goods in Brazil to a predetermined ordering company.

Before contracting an operation, it is important to assess:

- who negotiates and purchases the goods abroad;
- which company funds the acquisition and operating expenses;
- how commercial, tax, and customs documents will be organized;
- the required Siscomex authorization and linkage;
- tariff classification, taxes, applicable incentives, and total landed cost;
- regulatory requirements, production lead time, transportation, and delivery.

## Which costs and requirements require attention?

Import taxes, the trading company's services, international freight, insurance, storage, domestic transportation, and product-specific requirements must all be considered in the total landed cost. The tax structure should be assessed on a case-by-case basis; it should not be the only factor in defining the import model.

For regular operations, the importer and the acquiring or ordering company that is a legal entity must observe the applicable Siscomex authorization and linkage requirements. The Federal Revenue Service states that the agreement between the parties must be attached to the operation dossier, in accordance with the applicable rules. Consult the official guidance on <a href="https://www.gov.br/receitafederal/pt-br/assuntos/aduana-e-comercio-exterior/manuais/despacho-de-importacao/topicos-1/importacao-por-conta-e-ordem-e-importacao-por-encomenda-1/vinculacao-entre-o-importador-e-o-adquirente-ou-encomendante-no-siscomex" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">linkage between the importer and the acquiring or ordering company in Siscomex</a>.

## Frequently asked questions about import by account and order and custom import

### Who pays the foreign supplier under each model?

In import by account and order, the financial resources come from the acquiring company, although the trading company may make the payment to the supplier as part of the operation. In custom import, the trading company purchases the goods with its own resources for resale to the predetermined ordering company.

### Does the acquiring or ordering company need Siscomex authorization?

For operations carried out by legal entities, the importer and the acquiring or ordering company must observe Siscomex authorization and linkage requirements. Specific rules and exceptions apply to individuals and should be assessed case by case.

### Does the trading company own the goods in both models?

No. In import by account and order, the acquiring company is the effective importer and the trading company provides the contracted service. In custom import, the trading company purchases the goods abroad and resells them to the ordering company after customs clearance.

### Can I choose the model only because of lower taxes?

No. The model must reflect the commercial, financial, and documentary reality of the transaction. Taxes and possible incentives should be analyzed within the complete structure and do not replace the legal requirements of each model.

### Is an agreement between the parties required?

Yes. The Federal Revenue Service provides for a prior agreement between the parties and rules for linkage and documentation in Siscomex. Its content and formalization must be compatible with the selected model.

### How can I determine the right model for my company?

Start by mapping who purchases abroad, who provides the resources, what the Brazilian sale relationship will be, and which requirements apply to the goods. A tax, customs, and logistics analysis helps define the appropriate structure before contracting the operation.
